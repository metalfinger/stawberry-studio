# Dream visual treatment — Abhishek

Project: `045f766b-efdb-445f-a888-19852e251bad`
Status: charcoal-collage treatment adopted; ten asset candidates generated and
awaiting human review. No storyboard cuts have been generated for this project.

## Directing principle

Do not cap the production by a target image count. Use as many storyboard frames
as the story needs, but make every frame carry new narrative, spatial, emotional
or continuity information. The current breakdown contains 22 purposeful cuts.
Abhishek's supplied photo is stored and
approved as an identity-only source. See `DREAM_SHOT_LIST.md` for the exact plan.

## Themes (interpretive, not claims about the dreamer)

- Adult responsibility is literally carried on the chest, then disappears at
  the moment the adult is returned to being a student.
- A deadline exists without an explained task: anxiety precedes meaning.
- Old Bangalore is remembered as space, sound and motion rather than exposition.
- The school is not a destination for the child but a role waiting for Abhishek.
- The waking amusement keeps the film uncanny and humane, not tragic or horrific.

## Approved visual language

**Handmade monochrome charcoal-and-cut-paper dream collage.** Use graphite and
charcoal linework, torn fibrous paper edges, visible paper grain, imperfect
registration, rubbed-out marks and spatial discontinuity. The result must be
non-photorealistic but identity-readable. Keep the palette black, white and
warm paper grey with no color accents.

The supplied collage image is a material-language reference only. Borrow its
charcoal, graphite, paper texture and handmade assembly. Do not copy its numbered
layout, maps, newsprint, green or red accents, faceless silhouette, or individual
compositions. Avoid generic fantasy fog, glossy digital painting, cinematic
photorealism, horror lighting, floating symbols, expository text and literal
psychoanalytic imagery.

Character reference sheets must be reusable production assets, not story frames:
a flat unprinted matte off-white paper field, no environment, scenery, horizon,
furniture, floor plane, dramatic cast shadows, labels, numbers or text. Show the
same identity, body proportions and wardrobe consistently across full-body front,
three-quarter, profile and back views plus useful expression details. Location
sheets instead use connected, story-driven directional coverage with recurring
landmarks so geography survives between cuts.

The previously generated silver-memory realistic take was rejected and remains
stored only as history. It is not a style reference and must not be selected or
silently reused.

## Expanded ideal coverage

### 1. The White Road

1. Empty wide road and wind establish remembered Bangalore.
2. Abhishek enters the wide frame already running.
3. Hero/poster wide: Abhishek and child move one way, autos move opposite.
4. Lateral full-body tracking view establishes carrier and direction.
5. Medium close view holds Abhishek's anxiety and the child's quietness together.

### 2. No Ride Stops

6. Wide intersection: available transport moves the wrong way.
7. Abhishek scans for a ride without an invented conversation or explicit hail.
8. An auto passes through foreground, briefly occluding him; he is still running.
9. Long road compression makes the destination feel no closer.

### 3. Nine O'Clock

10. First reveal of stone arches beyond the trees.
11. Wide school exterior and clock tower; evening has become morning.
12. Clock insert: exactly 9:00 AM.
13. Profile composition includes Abhishek looking up and the clock in one frame.
14. Rear three-quarter approach beneath the large stone arches.

### 4. The Door Is for You

15. Full-body locked frame immediately before the threshold.
16. Detail of child and carrier, preserving their last confirmed state.
17. Matched threshold frame: Abhishek crosses with child and ordinary clothing.
18. Exact matched aftermath: same adult, school uniform, no child or carrier.
19. Medium confusion: Abhishek checks the now-empty chest area and looks around.

### 5. The Scene Collapses

20. Wider school interior/exterior space feels subtly oversized around him.
21. Architecture, clock geometry and film grain begin losing alignment.
22. The uniformed Abhishek remains briefly intact as the entire grey scene
    collapses and the image runs out. Do not show the waking world.

## Reference-development plan

1. **Style reference:** handmade monochrome charcoal, graphite, torn paper and
   controlled dream distortion. It governs material language without donating
   scene content or layout.
2. **Abhishek identity sheet:** use supplied photos; exact face, hair, build and
   age. Neutral full-body front/profile/back plus face detail. Do not invent a
   temporary prop or school uniform in the identity sheet.
3. **Abhishek wardrobe states:** ordinary running clothes and school uniform as
   separate approved states derived from the identity reference.
4. **Toddler + carrier relationship sheet:** scale, safe chest position, quiet
   neutral state and relevant side/front views. Use the actual child's likeness
   only if explicitly requested and appropriately authorized.
5. **Old Bangalore location pack:** white-road geography, intersection/traffic
   view and reverse direction with consistent trees and period street language.
6. **School location pack:** connected exterior, clock tower, arch approach,
   threshold and immediate interior. Treat the real named school literally only
   if the user chooses that; otherwise create a fictional dream analogue.
7. **Traffic design reference:** period auto-rickshaw and two-wheeler language.
   Do not force every background vehicle to be the identical prop.

After human approval of the generated asset candidates, select exact references
and generate the first hero cut from the style, Abhishek,
toddler/carrier and road references. Subsequent recipes choose only the references
needed by each cut and may reuse any approved earlier cut, including non-adjacent
ones. Preserve every version and show outputs without automatic visual critique.
